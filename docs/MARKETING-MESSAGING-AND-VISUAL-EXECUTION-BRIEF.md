## 2. Product truth: what marketing may and may not claim

### Supported today

- A selected regulatory library with official publications, full-text records, summaries, and official index records.
- Source-linked Answer sessions with citations, caveats, abstention rules, and saved conversations.
- Prepare sessions that create internal drafts with evidence requirements and an explicit review state.
- Firm workspaces, client records, tasks, roles, reviewer approval, and audit events.
- A source-linked calendar combining recurring general obligations and selected effective dates.
- An isolated public sample workspace using sample clients and sample statuses.

### Not a current production capability

- Comprehensive or continuous discovery of every new regulatory publication.
- Automatic professional conclusions about which clients are affected.
- Transaction-level applicability from Tally or live client ledgers.
- Live GST, MCA, Income Tax, FSSAI, or other portal status.
- Sending, filing, paying, submitting, or changing an external system.
- Complete central, state, labour, factory, licence, or sector coverage.
- Production-ready self-serve billing and onboarding.

### Claim rules

- Say **selected official sources**, not “every official source”.
- Say **clients that may be affected**, not “the right clients” or “exactly who is affected”.
- Say **prepare an internal draft**, not “execute an action”.
- Say **source linked**, **full text indexed**, or **source page reachable**. Never collapse these into “verified”.
- Say **sample workspace**, not “live demo”.
- Say **request pilot access**, not “start now”.
- “Prepared” must never imply “sent”, “submitted”, “filed”, or “complete”.

## 3. Audience and job to be done

### Primary buyer

A partner or managing partner at an Indian CA firm serving enough clients that one regulatory update can create several different applicability decisions.

### Day-to-day champion

A tax or compliance manager, senior associate, or reviewer who:

- searches circulars and notifications;
- explains them internally;
- checks client facts;
- decides what requires follow-up;
- prepares briefs, checklists, tasks, or calendar changes;
- sends work to a partner for review.

### Core job

> When a regulatory publication changes or a compliance position needs review, help me determine what the source says, whether it may affect a client, and what work should be prepared—without losing the source or overstating the conclusion.

### Desired outcomes

- Reduce repeated research across the firm.
- Standardise how evidence, caveats, and missing facts are recorded.
- Move from a generic update to a client-specific review.
- Preserve the trail from source to draft to reviewer.
- Help the firm advise earlier without asking it to trust opaque output.

### Anti-positioning

Do not present Reg Mitra as:

- return-filing software;
- a complete CA practice-management suite;
- a client CRM or document-management product;
- an enterprise GRC platform;
- an autonomous “AI CA”;
- a source of tax, legal, or accounting advice for the public.

## 4. Message hierarchy

Every page should earn the next layer. Do not repeat the hero sentence five times.

1. **Outcome:** A regulatory update becomes review-ready client work.
2. **Mechanism:** The official source, recorded client context, and next draft stay connected.
3. **Trust:** Evidence gaps and uncertainty remain visible.
4. **Control:** A professional controls the conclusion; no external action occurs.
5. **Scope:** The library and workflow are selected and pilot-stage.
6. **Conversion:** Explore the sample, then request a focused pilot.

### Voice

- Calm, exact, and practitioner-to-practitioner.
- Plain English before product terminology.
- Short sentences and concrete artifacts.
- Evidence-led, not AI-led.
- Commercially confident without pretending uncertainty is gone.
- Indian/British spelling: authorised, personalised, licence, cancelled.

## 5.7 FAQ

**Kicker**

> Before you try Reg Mitra

**Headline**

> What the pilot does—and what it does not.

**Lede**

> Clear answers about sources, coverage, client data, and external actions.

### 1. What is Reg Mitra?

Reg Mitra is a source-grounded regulatory research and client-impact review workspace for Indian CA firms. It helps a team research an official update, compare it with recorded client facts, and prepare a draft for professional review.

### 2. Who is the current pilot for?

The pilot is best suited to CA firm partners, tax and compliance managers, and teams that handle regulatory research across multiple clients. It is not a filing utility or a replacement for a complete practice-management system.

### 3. What regulatory material is included?

The current library contains a selected set of official publications, summaries, and index records covering CBIC, GSTN, CBDT and the Income Tax Department, MCA, RBI, SEBI, ICAI, EPFO, and FSSAI. Coverage is not complete, and it does not yet promise every circular, state rule, or update.

### 4. Does Reg Mitra monitor every official source each day?

No. The current pilot performs daily availability checks for selected calendar sources and uses a curated regulatory library. Full scheduled discovery and continuous coverage across every authority are still in development.

### 5. How does it decide which clients are affected?

Reg Mitra uses recorded client context and open work to structure an applicability review. The result is a prompt to review, not an automatic professional conclusion. Missing or conflicting facts remain visible until a reviewer resolves them.

### 6. What is the difference between Answer and Prepare?

Answer researches a question using the selected source library and shows citations, caveats, and missing evidence. Prepare creates an internal draft brief, checklist, proposed calendar change, document pack, or client note with an explicit approval state.

### 7. Can Prepare send, submit, or file anything?

No. In the current pilot, Prepare creates a draft only. It cannot send email or WhatsApp messages, file a return, make a payment, enter an OTP, or change a government portal—even after a draft is approved.

### 8. Does Reg Mitra connect to Tally or government portals?

Not in the current pilot. Tally, GST, MCA, and Income Tax connections are planned, but no live ledger or portal status should be assumed today. Never paste passwords, portal credentials, CAPTCHAs, or OTPs into Reg Mitra.

### 9. Is Reg Mitra legal, tax, or accounting advice?

No. It is a research and preparation aid for qualified professionals. Applicability, filing positions, client communications, and other professional conclusions require appropriate review.

### 10. Can I use real client data in the sample workspace?

No. The public sample uses sample records only. Do not enter confidential client information there. Real data belongs only in a firm workspace issued for the pilot.

### 11. How does the seven-day pilot work?

Request access from your work email. We confirm the workflow and setup, then create the workspace; the seven days begin at creation. No card is required, there is no automatic charge, and pricing is confirmed before any paid plan.

### 12. Are the dates and statuses in the sample current?

No. Sample clients, dates, statuses, conversations, and portal results demonstrate the review flow. They must not be used as current regulatory or client evidence.

The visual must show the product’s reasoning chain, not decorate the page with a compliance metaphor.
